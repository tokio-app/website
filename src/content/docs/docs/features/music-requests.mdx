Viewers request songs from chat. YouTube songs play in Tokio's own player window, and Spotify songs in your Spotify app.

## Providers

Pick one under **Provider** on the **Music** page, then turn on **Music** under **Active features** on **Home**. Music doesn't reconnect when Tokio starts, so click **Connect** each time you open it.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="YouTube">

A free Google account works, but ads play between songs and can delay them or cut them short. **YouTube Premium** or **YouTube Music Premium** skips them.

<Steps>

1. Pick **YouTube** and click **Connect**.
2. Sign in to Google in the window that opens. Tokio remembers it.

</Steps>

<Screenshot
  name="music-provider"
  alt="The Provider section with YouTube picked"
/>

Keep the player window open while you stream: closing it disconnects music.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Spotify">

You need **Spotify Premium**: only Premium accounts can let another app control playback. Tokio connects through an app of your own, made once in Spotify's dashboard.

<Steps>

1. Log in to the [Spotify Developer Dashboard](https://developer.spotify.com/dashboard).
2. Create an app. Any name works, like "Tokio".
3. Add this redirect URI, exactly: `http://127.0.0.1:5173/callback`
4. If it asks which APIs you'll use, check **Web API**.
5. Accept the terms and save.
6. In the app's **Settings**, copy the **Client ID**. You don't need the secret.

</Steps>

Then, on Tokio's **Music** page:

<Steps>

1. Pick **Spotify** and paste the ID in **Spotify Client ID**.
2. Click **Connect**, log in and let Tokio control your playback.

</Steps>

<Screenshot
  name="music-spotify"
  alt="The Provider section with Spotify picked and its Client ID field"
/>

Before your LIVE, open Spotify and play a song, so Tokio has a device to play on. Connect with the account that made the app, or add others to its users in the dashboard first, up to five.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Chat commands

| Command   | Action                                   |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| `!play`   | Request a song                           |
| `!next`   | Play the next waiting request            |
| `!last`   | Go back to the last song                 |
| `!revoke` | Remove a song from the queue by its name |

<Screenshot
  name="music-commands"
  alt="The Controls section, where each chat command can be renamed or turned off"
/>

Rename or turn off each one under **Controls**; viewers can type them in any case.

`!play One More Time` adds that song, and `!revoke` takes its name as typed or as it shows in the queue. A song that's playing or already played can't be revoked.

A request waits for the song playing to end. If you skip that song in the player window or in Spotify, the next request starts within a minute. A request without a song name, one that finds nothing, or one past **Max queue length** is ignored, and `!next` does nothing with no request waiting.

**Limit Next, Last and Revoke**, on by default, keeps `!next`, `!last` and revoking other viewers' songs to you and your moderators; turn it off to open them to every viewer **Permissions** allows. With it on, viewers can still skip a song they asked for while it plays, and revoke their own requests.

## Who can request

Music has its own **Permissions** and **Protection**, set like [text-to-speech's](/docs/features/text-to-speech/#permissions). The cooldown is for all of chat: a command sent before it passes is dropped.

<Screenshot
  name="music-protection"
  alt="The Protection section on the Music page, with One request per viewer on"
/>

**One request per viewer** ignores a viewer's next `!play` while their song waits, and doesn't add a song that's already playing or waiting.

Song changes are a LIVE event too, so they can trigger an [alert](/docs/features/alerts/) or update an [overlay](/docs/features/overlays/).
