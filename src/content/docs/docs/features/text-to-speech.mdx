<Steps>

1. On the **TTS** page, click the voice and pick one from the list.
2. Set the speed, pitch and volume, type a message and press **Test voice**.

   <Screenshot
     name="tts-voice"
     alt="The voice, its speed, pitch and volume, and Test voice with a message"
   />

3. On **Home**, turn on **TTS** under **Active features**.

</Steps>

**Test voice** applies your replacements, **Remove emojis** and banned words, so a message with a banned word or nothing left to read shows a notice instead.

The format, the length limit and the permissions don't apply to the test, so it can play a message chat would skip.

## Voices

| Engine         | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **TikTok**     | TikTok's voices. They go through `ottsy.weilbyte.dev`, a proxy run by a third party, which gets the text of every message they read. Add your TikTok session ID in **Settings → Voice engines** to skip it.                                      |
| **Edge**       | Microsoft Edge's voices.                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Windows**    | The voices installed on Windows, SAPI and OneCore, 32-bit ones included.                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **ElevenLabs** | The voices in your ElevenLabs account, cloned ones included, with your own API key in **Settings → Voice engines**. They speak all of ElevenLabs' languages and use your account's credits. ElevenLabs gets the text of every message they read. |

Search the list, filter it by engine and language, and play any voice before you pick it.

## Clone your voice

Have chat read in your own voice, cloned with ElevenLabs. Cloning needs a paid ElevenLabs plan.

<Steps>

1. Create an API key in your ElevenLabs account and paste it in **Settings → Voice engines**. Give the key access to Text to Speech, and read and write access to Voices.
2. On the **TTS** page, click **Clone a voice**, the microphone next to **Voice**. It shows once your ElevenLabs key is set. Name the voice and add samples: audio files, or a recording you make there with **Record**.
3. Check that the voice is yours or that you have the speaker's permission, then click **Clone voice**.
4. Give the new voice to a special user under [Special users](#special-users), and keep a free voice for the rest of chat. It also works for all of chat or an alert's message, but every message it reads spends credits.

</Steps>

Samples work best with 1 to 2 minutes of one person speaking clearly, without music or background noise. A recording stops by itself at 3 minutes.

<details>
<summary>Credits, limits and deleting a voice</summary>

Every message an ElevenLabs voice reads spends credits from your account. On a busy LIVE, reading all of chat can spend 30,000 credits in an hour or less, while one special user's messages last many LIVEs. To cap what a LIVE can spend, set a credit limit on the API key in ElevenLabs. When the credits run out, Tokio tells you, and the voice stays quiet until you add more or pick another one.

A voice takes up to 10 samples of up to 25 MB each: MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG, FLAC or WebM files. Tokio sends them only to ElevenLabs and doesn't keep them. To rename or delete a cloned voice, use the ElevenLabs website.

</details>

## Special users

Give some viewers a voice of their own: under **Special users**, add their @handle and pick a voice for each. Their messages still go through the format, permissions and protection, and are read at the same speed, pitch and volume as everyone's.

## Format

Read every message, or only the ones that start with a dot, a slash or a command of your own.

Choosing **Custom command** fills in `!tts`, which you can change. The command isn't read aloud, and with the command left blank nothing is read.

<Screenshot
  name="tts-format"
  alt="The Format section: which messages get read, the message template and Remove emojis"
/>

The **Message template** sets what's read:

| Placeholder  | Replaced with                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| `{username}` | The viewer's display name            |
| `{handle}`   | The viewer's handle, without the `@` |
| `{comment}`  | The chat message                     |

`{username} {comment}` reads "Lucia hi from Lima!". **Remove emojis** leaves emojis out, and skips messages that are only emojis.

A message is skipped when the filled template has no letter, number or emoji, like `{comment}` with "?" alone.

## Replacements

Teach the voice to say acronyms, slang and names. Each one swaps a whole word, in any case, and applies before **Remove emojis**, so ❤️ can become "love".

## Permissions

Uncheck **Allow everyone**, then check **Followers only**, **Super Fans only**, **Moderators only** or **Minimum fan level**. Until you check one, everyone still talks, and a viewer who matches any checked one gets through.

With **Use allow and block lists**, an allow list with names lets only them talk. @handles on the block list are never read, even when they're on the allow list.

## Protection

The **Cooldown** is the seconds between two messages, for all of chat. Messages that come while the queue holds **Max queue length** messages are dropped.

Messages over the **Max message length**, or with a banned word in the comment, name or @handle, are skipped, even with look-alike letters or hidden characters. A banned word also matches inside other words, so banning "ass" skips "class" too.

<Screenshot
  name="tts-protection"
  alt="The Protection section: cooldown, maximum queue length, maximum comment length and banned words"
/>

Replacements, **Remove emojis**, banned words and the block list also apply to [alerts that speak](/docs/features/alerts/#spoken-messages). [Music](/docs/features/music-requests/#who-can-request) has its own permissions.
