Overlay Studio builds your own overlays from text, images, your cameras, gifts and the overlays you already have. Each element can stay on screen or react to your LIVE, and each overlay is one browser source.

<Steps>

1. In **Tools**, open **Overlay Studio** and click **New overlay**. It's named **Overlay 1**, or the next free number, and opens in the editor.
2. Click **+** in **Elements** and pick what to show. Drag it into place on the canvas.
3. Click **Copy overlay URL** and add it to your streaming app (see [Adding an overlay](/docs/guides/adding-an-overlay/)):

   ```
   http://tokio-app.local:37891/studio/<overlay-id>/
   ```

</Steps>

Changes save as you make them and reach your stream right away. Every profile shares the same overlays, but what you [embed](#embedding-overlays) from alerts, the countdown and the games overlay follows the active profile.

New overlays are **Portrait**, 1080 × 1920. With nothing selected, **Overlay settings** changes the name and the **Canvas**, to **Landscape** at 1920 × 1080.

The browser source scales the canvas to fit, centered.

## Elements

An overlay is made of elements, the things that show:

| Element              | What it shows                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Text**             | Your text, with its **Font**, **Size**, **Color**, **Alignment**, **Bold** and **Dark halo**. See [Text](#text).                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Image or video**   | A file from the media library you use for [alerts](/docs/features/alerts/#images-and-videos). A video plays with its sound.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Camera**           | A camera on this computer, live. See [Cameras](#cameras).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Gift**             | The picture of the gift a viewer sends, or of one gift you pick. The gift sent needs the **Gift** trigger, so it can't be **Always on screen** or **Moves all the time**.                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Emote**            | The emote a viewer sends in chat, or one you pick from those viewers have used. The emote sent needs the **Emote** trigger, so it can't be **Always on screen** or **Moves all the time**.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Shape**            | A rectangle, an ellipse, a polygon, a star, an arrow, a heart or a speech bubble, filling its box, with a **Fill** and a **Border**, each in its own color. Turn either one off. **Border width** goes from 1 to 100. A polygon has 3 to 12 **Sides**, a star 3 to 12 **Points**, and a rectangle or speech bubble a **Corner radius**. |
| **Viewer's picture** | The profile picture of the viewer who set the element off, or their initial when they have none. It can't be **Always on screen** or **Moves all the time**, and only reacts to events a viewer sends: not an envelope, a Super Fan Box or a music change.                                                                              |
| **Overlay**          | An installed overlay, the countdown, the games overlay or an alert's overlay. See [Embedding overlays](#embedding-overlays).                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

To order, lock, hide and move elements together, put them in a group. The [Overlay Studio editor](/docs/features/overlay-studio-editor/) page covers groups, placing, cropping and the keyboard shortcuts.

A font that isn't installed on the computer that runs your streaming app shows as Archivo, and a browser tab plays a video's sound only after a click on it. See [Fonts and sound](/docs/guides/adding-an-overlay/#fonts-and-sound).

If you delete a file from the media library, it also leaves every element that showed it, and those elements stay empty.

## When

Each element's and group's **When** sets how it reacts:

| When                   | What it does                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Always on screen**   | Stays on screen.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Shows on an event**  | Stays hidden until its **Trigger**, then comes in with its **Entrance** for its **Seconds on screen**, from 1 to 60, and leaves with its **Exit**. |
| **Moves on an event**  | Stays on screen and plays its **Animation** each time its **Trigger** fires.                                                                       |
| **Moves all the time** | Stays on screen and plays its **Animation** over and over, with no trigger.                                                                        |

**Entrance** and **Exit** each pick one of Fade, Pop, Slide up, Slide down, Slide left, Slide right, Zoom, Drop, Spin, Flip, Wipe and Blur. For **Moves on an event**, **Animation** picks one of Bounce, Shake, Pulse, Wiggle, Spin, Flip, Jump, Swing, Ta-da, Heartbeat, Flash and Jelly; for **Moves all the time**, one of Float, Breathe, Spin, Sway, Hop and Drift.

A group moves as one: one that shows on an event brings all its elements in and out together, and one that moves carries them with it. Its elements keep their own **When**, so an element can bounce on each gift while its group floats.

The **Trigger** picker has the same events and filters as [alerts](/docs/features/alerts/#triggers). On **Like**, the element reacts every time your viewers send another 100 likes in total; change that number in the field next to the trigger.

If its trigger fires again while it shows or leaves, it starts over with the newest event's values.

In the editor, an element or group that shows on an event stays on the canvas with a dashed outline, so you can place it. What moves all the time moves on the canvas too.

## Text

A text element can show live numbers from your LIVE, written in braces:

| Variable      | Shows                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------- |
| `{likes}`     | The LIVE's likes                |
| `{viewers}`   | The viewers watching now        |
| `{followers}` | New followers during this LIVE  |
| `{shares}`    | Shares during this LIVE         |
| `{coins}`     | Coins received during this LIVE |

They're shortened in Tokio's language (**Settings → Language**), like 12.8K in English, and read 0 while you're not LIVE. The editor shows sample numbers.

A text that reacts to an event can also use that event's values, like `Thanks {username}!` on a gift or `{username} sent {count} {gift}`. Like has no viewer or count of its own, since its likes come from every viewer: `{likes}` shows the LIVE's total.

The variables a text can use are listed under its **Text**: the LIVE numbers, then the event's when it reacts to one.

An element keeps its last event's values until Tokio closes, so `Last follower: {username}` still shows after you reload the browser source. Before its first event, those variables are empty.

Hidden elements, and the elements of a hidden group, keep reacting, so they show the latest event when you show them again.

## Cameras

A **Camera** element shows a camera on this computer, live, on your stream and on the canvas. In **Camera**, pick **Default camera** (the one your system uses first) or any camera the computer has.

Each element can show a different camera, and several elements can show the same one. A camera you picked that isn't plugged in shows as **(not connected)**.

**Quality** sends the camera at **720p** or **1080p**. 1080p is sharper and takes more of your computer's power; 720p is plenty for a camera in a corner.

Elements that show the same camera share it at the higher of their qualities.

Crop it and round its corners like a picture, up to a circle for a round camera, and use **Flip** to mirror it. To show a camera on its own, make an overlay with one **Camera** element that fills the canvas.

Tokio turns a camera on only while the editor or an overlay that shows it is open, and turns it off a few seconds after the last one closes. Hiding the element turns its camera off too.

The editor shows the camera straight from your computer, so it works with the overlay server off. Your stream gets it through the server.

:::note
Once a camera has been on, Tokio uses about 100 MB more memory until you quit it, depending on your camera and graphics card. Chromium, which Tokio is built on, keeps that memory for the next camera, so it doesn't keep growing.
:::

Other computers on your network can't open your camera: only the overlays Tokio serves get its address, which changes every time the overlay server starts.

If a camera can't start, Tokio tells you why and tries again every 5 seconds:

- Another app may be using it, like OBS's own **Video Capture Device** for the same camera. Use the camera in one app at a time.
- On Windows, turn on **Let desktop apps access your camera** in **Settings → Privacy & security → Camera**.
- On macOS, allow Tokio when it asks, or later in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera**.

:::tip
The camera can reach your stream slightly behind your microphone. If your lips look out of sync, delay the microphone a little in your streaming app. In OBS, that's **Sync Offset** in **Advanced Audio Properties**.
:::

## Embedding overlays

An **Overlay** element shows another overlay inside this one: pick it in **Overlay**. The list has your installed overlays, the **Countdown**, the **Games overlay**, and the alerts of the active profile that show an image or video.

If your games overlay has more than one column, each column is in the list too (**Games overlay, column 2**, or **Games overlay, row 2** when it's horizontal), so you can place them apart. **Games overlay** shows all of them in one box.

On your stream, each one is the real overlay at its own address, live. In the editor:

- an installed overlay shows its own sample data;
- the countdown and the games overlay show the previews from their own pages;
- an alert's overlay is a box with the alert's name, since it only shows its image or video while the alert plays.

The countdown, the games overlay and alerts follow the active profile. An alert from another profile shows nothing, and the editor calls it **Deleted alert**.

If you remove an official overlay you embedded, its element shows nothing until you install it again.

## Test

The play button, **Test the elements and groups that react to events**, plays each element and group that reacts once, elements with sample values, in the editor and on your stream. An element that shows on an event plays its entrance, its seconds on screen and its exit, then comes back to the canvas.

Embedded overlays don't get the samples. Tokio keeps them only while their element is on screen, so after that a reload shows the real last event again.

## Notes

<details>
<summary>An overlay embedded twice shares its settings</summary>

An overlay you embed in two elements, or in two Overlay Studio overlays, has one set of settings, the same as when you add it twice as browser sources.

</details>

<details>
<summary>The editor's timer and games previews are close, not exact</summary>

The editor can't open the real countdown or games overlay, so it scales their pages' previews to the element's box. Check them in your streaming app.

</details>
