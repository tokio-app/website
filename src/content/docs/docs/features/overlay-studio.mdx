Build your own overlays from text, images, cameras, gifts and the overlays you already have. Each one is a single browser source.

<Steps>

1. In **Tools**, open **Overlay Studio** and click **New overlay**. It opens in the editor.
2. Click **+** in **Elements** and pick what to show. Drag it into place on the canvas.
3. Click **Copy overlay URL** and add it to your streaming app (see [Adding an overlay](/docs/guides/adding-an-overlay/)):

   ```
   http://tokio-app.local:37891/studio/<overlay-id>/
   ```

</Steps>

Changes save as you make them and reach your stream right away. Every profile shares the same overlays, but what you [embed](#embedding-overlays) from alerts, the countdown and the games overlay follows the active profile.

New overlays are **Portrait**, 1080 × 1920. For **Landscape**, click an empty spot and change **Canvas** in **Overlay settings**. Your streaming app scales the canvas to fit.

Each overlay's row has **Duplicate overlay**, for a copy with its own URL, and **Delete overlay**.

## Elements

An overlay is made of elements, the things that show:

| Element              | What it shows                                                                                                                |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Text**             | Your text, with live numbers and the event's values. See [Text](#text).                                                      |
| **Image or video**   | A file from the media library you use for [alerts](/docs/features/alerts/#images-and-videos). A video plays with its sound.  |
| **Camera**           | A camera on this computer, live. See [Cameras](#cameras).                                                                    |
| **Gift**             | The picture of the gift a viewer sends, or a TikTok gift you pick. TikTok and YouTube only.                                  |
| **Emote**            | The emote a viewer sends in chat, or one you pick from those your viewers have used.                                         |
| **Shape**            | A rectangle, ellipse, polygon, star, arrow, heart or speech bubble, with a **Fill**, a **Border** or both.                   |
| **Viewer's picture** | The profile picture of the viewer who set it off, or their initial.                                                          |
| **Goal bar**         | A bar that fills as a LIVE number or a [counter](#counters) comes to its goal. See [Goal bars](#goal-bars).                  |
| **List**             | Rows of this LIVE's top gifters or top gifted subs, or its newest followers, gifts or chat messages. See [Lists](#lists).    |
| **Overlay**          | An installed overlay, the countdown, the games overlay or an alert's overlay. See [Embedding overlays](#embedding-overlays). |

To order, lock, hide and move elements together, put them in a group. The [Overlay Studio editor](/docs/features/overlay-studio-editor/) page covers groups, placing, cropping and the keyboard shortcuts.

A font that isn't installed on the computer that runs your streaming app shows as Archivo, and a browser tab plays a video's sound only after a click on it. See [Fonts and sound](/docs/guides/adding-an-overlay/#fonts-and-sound).

Every element also has a **Shadow** and a **Blur**. With **X** and **Y** at 0, the shadow is a glow.

## When

Each element's and group's **When** sets how it reacts:

| When                   | What it does                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Always on screen**   | Stays on screen.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Shows on an event**  | Stays hidden until its **Trigger**, then comes in with its **Entrance** for its **Seconds on screen**, from 1 to 60, and leaves with its **Exit**. |
| **Moves on an event**  | Stays on screen and plays its **Animation** each time its **Trigger** fires.                                                                       |
| **Moves all the time** | Stays on screen and plays its **Animation** over and over, with no trigger.                                                                        |

<Screenshot
  name="studio-when"
  alt="The follow message's When: Shows on an event, on Follow, with Pop as its entrance, Slide down as its exit, 5 seconds on screen and no delay"
/>

A group reacts as one, while its elements keep their own **When**: an element can bounce on each gift while its group floats.

The **Trigger** picker has the same events, filters and [minimums](/docs/features/alerts/#minimums) as [alerts](/docs/features/alerts/#triggers), without **From**. On **Like**, the element reacts every time your viewers send another 100 likes in total; change that number in the field next to the trigger.

**Add a trigger** adds another one under **Or**, up to 5 in all, each with its own filters. The element reacts to any of them.

**Goal reached** reacts once per LIVE, the first time a number reaches its goal. Pick a LIVE number, like `{likes}` or `{coins}`, or one of the overlay's [counters](#counters), then the goal.

**Delay (s)** makes an element or group that shows or moves on an event wait up to 10 seconds after its trigger, for example until an alert is over.

## Text

A text element can show live numbers from your LIVE, written in braces:

| Variable       | Shows                                                |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `{likes}`      | The LIVE's likes                                     |
| `{viewers}`    | The viewers watching now                             |
| `{followers}`  | New followers during this LIVE                       |
| `{shares}`     | Shares during this LIVE                              |
| `{coins}`      | TikTok coins received during this LIVE               |
| `{kicks}`      | Kicks received during this LIVE                      |
| `{bits}`       | Bits received during this LIVE                       |
| `{superchats}` | Super Chats received during this LIVE, in US dollars |
| `{gifts}`      | Gifts received during this LIVE                      |
| `{giftsubs}`   | Subs gifted during this LIVE                         |
| `{uptime}`     | How long you've been LIVE, like 01:02:03             |

They're shortened, like 12.8K, and read 0 while you're not LIVE. A text also shows the overlay's [counters](#counters), like `{roses}`.

A text that reacts to an event can also use its values, like `Thanks {username}!`. Click a variable under **Text** to add it. On Chat and Emote, `{translation}` shows the message's [translation](/docs/features/chat-translation/).

<Screenshot
  name="studio-text"
  alt="A text's settings: the follow message, with the LIVE numbers and the event's values listed under it to add"
/>

An element keeps its last event's values until Tokio closes, so `Last follower: {username}` still shows after you reload the browser source. Hidden elements keep reacting too.

<details>
<summary>Text styles</summary>

**Gradient** fades a text's color into a second one, at the angle you set: 90 runs left to right, 180 top to bottom. **Outline** draws a line around the letters, outside them, in its own color and width. **Letter spacing** and **Line height** are percents of the size, and line height starts at 115. A thick outline can reach past the text's box.

</details>

## Counters

A counter counts an event during each LIVE, like roses or follows. With nothing selected, click **Add a counter** in **Overlay settings**. Name it, like `roses`, and pick what it counts, with the same filters as a trigger. Each overlay has up to 10 of its own.

A text shows it as `{roses}`, and **Goal reached** can wait for it. What each event adds:

- A **Gift** adds the gifts sent: with **When streak ends**, a whole streak when it ends; with **Every gift**, each one as it comes.
- A **Like** adds its likes.
- Any other event adds 1.

Counters start at 0 when you connect, keep counting when Tokio recovers a dropped connection, and read 0 while you're not LIVE.

A name takes letters without accents, numbers and `_`, up to 24 characters.

## Goal bars

A **Goal bar** fills as its **Number** comes to its **Goal**. The number is a LIVE number, like `{likes}` or `{coins}`, or one of the overlay's [counters](#counters). The bar is full past its goal, and empty while you're not LIVE.

A bar shows no words: put a text on or next to it, like `{roses} / 10 roses`.

## Lists

A **List** shows a row per viewer or event of what it **Shows**:

| Shows                         | Its rows                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Top gifters this LIVE**     | The viewers who sent the most this LIVE on its **Platform**, in its money: coins, Bits, Kicks or Super Chats. |
| **Top gifted subs this LIVE** | The viewers who gifted the most subs this LIVE, on Twitch, YouTube or Kick, the most first                    |
| **New followers**             | The newest followers, the newest first. One who follows again moves up                                        |
| **Latest gifts**              | The newest gifts, with the gift and its count, the newest first. A streak shows when it ends                  |
| **Chat**                      | The newest messages, the newest at the bottom. Messages of emotes alone don't show                            |

Each row shows the viewer's picture and name, with amounts on the right (YouTube's in US dollars). **Rows** sets how many show, up to 10.

**Space between rows** sets the gap and **Number color** the amounts' color. Turn off **Viewers' pictures** to show names alone.

Lists are empty while you're not LIVE and start over when you connect.

<details>
<summary>Which viewers and platforms a list shows</summary>

- A top lists only the viewers who sent some of its money or gifted subs, so YouTube's Gifts, which have no price, never put their senders on it.
- With a platform besides TikTok in the profile, a top picks its **Platform**. **New followers** shows only with a platform that sends follows, and **Latest gifts** with one that sends gifts.
- What doesn't fit in the box is cut off at its edge, chat's oldest at the top.

</details>

## Cameras

A **Camera** element shows a camera on this computer, live. Pick it in **Camera**; one that isn't plugged in shows as **(not connected)**.

**Quality** is **720p**, plenty for a corner, or **1080p**, sharper but heavier on your computer.

Crop it and round its corners like a picture, up to a circle for a round camera, and use **Flip** to mirror it. To show a camera on its own, make an overlay with one **Camera** element that fills the canvas.

<Screenshot
  name="studio-camera"
  alt="A camera cropped to a circle on the canvas, with its settings: Default camera at 720p"
/>

Tokio turns a camera on only while the editor or an overlay that shows it is open, and turns it off a few seconds after the last one closes. Hiding the element turns its camera off too.

:::note
Once a camera has been on, Tokio uses about 100 MB more memory until you quit it.
:::

:::tip
If your lips look out of sync, delay your microphone a little in your streaming app (in OBS, **Sync Offset** in **Advanced Audio Properties**).
:::

<details>
<summary>If a camera doesn't show</summary>

If a camera can't start, Tokio tries again every 5 seconds, and tells you why once your stream uses it:

- Another app may be using it, like OBS's own **Video Capture Device** for the same camera. Use the camera in one app at a time.
- On Windows, turn on **Let desktop apps access your camera** in **Settings → Privacy & security → Camera**.
- On macOS, allow Tokio when it asks, or later in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera**.

The editor shows the camera straight from your computer, so it works with the overlay server off. Your stream gets it through the server.

Other computers on your network can't open your camera: only the overlays Tokio serves get its address, which changes every time the overlay server starts.

</details>

## Embedding overlays

An **Overlay** element shows another overlay inside this one: pick it in **Overlay**. The list has your installed overlays, the **Countdown**, the **Games overlay**, and the alerts of the active profile that show an image or video.

If your games overlay has more than one column, each column is in the list too (**Games overlay, column 2**, or **Games overlay, row 2** when it's horizontal), so you can place them apart. **Games overlay** shows all of them in one box.

On your stream, each one is the real overlay at its own address, live. An alert from another profile shows nothing, and the editor calls it **Deleted alert**.

If you remove an official overlay you embedded, its element shows nothing until you install it again.

## Test

The shooting-star button, **Test the elements and groups that react to events**, plays every element and group that reacts once, with sample values, in the editor and on your stream. It's off while nothing reacts to events.

The samples come from the profile's platforms, so one event's elements show the same viewer, and a top's rows are its platform's. Embedded overlays don't get them.

## Notes

<details>
<summary>Deleted files and other profiles</summary>

If you delete a file from the media library, it also leaves every element that showed it, and those elements stay empty.

The editor offers only what the active profile's platforms send: the triggers, the LIVE numbers and goals, the lists and the emotes. What an overlay already has from another platform stays until you change it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>When a reaction waits or starts over</summary>

- Another trigger during an element's **Delay** changes the event it shows, but doesn't restart the wait.
- If its trigger fires again while it shows or leaves, it starts over with the newest event's values.
- On **Like**, the count starts over when you disconnect.
- If you change the goal of **Goal reached**, it can react again in the same LIVE.
- An element that shows on an event plays its entrance, its seconds on screen and its exit on **Test**, after its delay, then comes back to the canvas.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What the editor shows</summary>

- LIVE numbers, counters, lists and goal bars show samples: each counter reads 12, and every bar shows 60% full.
- An installed overlay shows its own sample data.
- The countdown and the games overlay show the previews from their own pages, scaled to the element's box. They're close, not exact: check them in your streaming app.
- An alert's overlay is a box with the alert's name, since it only shows its image or video while the alert plays.

</details>

<details>
<summary>An overlay embedded twice shares its settings</summary>

An overlay you embed in two elements, or in two Overlay Studio overlays, has one set of settings, the same as when you add it twice as browser sources.

</details>
